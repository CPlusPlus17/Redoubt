Privacy & limits

Redoubt’s public summary of what it protects, and what it does and does not mean.

What data Redoubt and its developer collect (none) and which connections the app makes is in the privacy policy.

Redoubt ships the same privacy configuration and the same Gecko-level security patches as LibreWolf desktop, on a platform whose process containment is weaker — and we publish exactly where.

“Where” is the parity table in PARITY.md: every row is backed by a named measurement or marked as not yet verified. This page is a summary of it; when they disagree, PARITY.md is right.

What it changes compared with Firefox for Android

  • Built from source. Gecko and the Android app are compiled from Firefox’s source with LibreWolf’s shared patch set and preference configuration applied.
  • Telemetry and experiments removed: the Glean upload path, Adjust, Nimbus experiments, crash-report uploads and Google Play Services are patched out; sponsored shortcuts are removed and search suggestions are off by default.
  • LibreWolf’s privacy defaults: resist-fingerprinting, strict Enhanced Tracking Protection and cookie partitioning (both locked), Global Privacy Control on. Resist-fingerprinting can be switched off in Settings → Privacy and security → Global privacy controls.
  • Stronger site isolation than stock: Fission is locked to isolate high-value sites; stock Firefox for Android ships with it off.
  • uBlock Origin preinstalled, with its cookie-notice lists on by default. A filter list hides a cookie banner or blocks its script; it does not click “reject”, so the site records no refusal.
  • WebGL and canvas readback need your approval per site, from the site information panel; browsing data is deleted when you quit, unless you turn that off.
  • Cloud features off, such as Shake to Summarize, IP Protection and Google Lens.

Honest limits

  • No content-process sandbox. Firefox’s Gecko content sandbox does not exist on Android (upstream never finished the port). Per-site process isolation and RLBox recover part of it. On Android 12 and newer, web pages run in Android’s isolated processes (since 158.0-2); H.264, HEVC and AAC video and audio are decoded in a separate, non-isolated decoder process, which is the remaining attack surface. On Android 8–11 isolated processes are not used. This is the largest containment gap versus desktop.
  • Not everything is proven on a live page. HTTPS-only, certificate revocation, the TLS floor and fingerprinting coherence are configured but marked Partial in PARITY.md until measured on a device.
  • DNS over HTTPS is set in the app. It is off by default, as in LibreWolf, and the app’s setting starts from LibreWolf’s values (Quad9 preselected, LibreWolf’s provider list). The app rewrites the DNS-over-HTTPS prefs on every start, so turn it on in Settings → DNS over HTTPS, not in about:config. Which resolver is used once it is on has not been measured yet.
  • Tested on emulators and two phones. Every release passes automated acceptance on Android 11, 12 and 14 emulators. On 2026-10-14 the signed 158.0-2 release (arm64) ran on a Pixel 9a (Android 17) and a Xiaomi Mi 8 (LineageOS, Android 15): web pages ran in isolated processes, 1080p H.264, VP9 and AV1 test videos played without dropped frames, common sites loaded, and nothing crashed. The maintainer also tested an arm64 development build on a Pixel 10 Pro (Android 17). Not tested yet: phones with 4 GB of RAM or less, Android 8–11 on real hardware, and real password managers.
  • One person holds the signing key. Redoubt cannot carry the desktop build’s code-signing identity; its trust root is its own app id and signing key. That key has one holder and no second key holder yet. Today the release key and the update-signing key are passphrase-encrypted files on the maintainer’s workstation, not on an offline machine; offline backups exist. Moving the keys to a hardware key (a YubiKey, from which the keys cannot be copied) is planned. CI builds the APKs on another machine and never has a key. The release key is never uploaded to Google.
  • Built largely by AI agents. AI coding agents write and check most of the code, patches, tests and documentation; the maintainer sets direction, decides the trade-offs, tests on devices and signs releases. See How Redoubt is made.
  • A small project. If you want a mature, widely used hardened Firefox for Android today, IronFox is that, and it is the browser LibreWolf itself recommends to Android users.
